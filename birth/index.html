Step 4

What women actually care about

Tap any topic to see how each facility handles it.

Will they respect my birth plan? +

Mercy: Physician-led; birth plans are generally honored within clinical judgment. Strong culture around reducing unnecessary interventions (ARPQC Platinum).

Willow Creek: Strongest culture around low-intervention, plan-honoring birth. The whole hospital is designed for this. Your plan is the point.

Washington Regional: Physician-led; honors plans but US News ranking comes from outcomes, not flexibility — some patients report a more clinical feel.

What to ask on tour: "Can I see a sample birth plan your team honored for a first-time mom last month?"

Pain management options (beyond epidural) +

Mercy: Full epidural service, 24/7 CRNA anesthesia, IV pain meds. Less emphasis on alternatives.

Willow Creek: Full epidural + nitrous oxide + labor tubs + hydrotherapy + TENS units + movement-friendly rooms. The most options for non-medicated pain management.

Washington Regional: Full epidural + some low-intervention options. Anesthesia staffing model not publicly confirmed.

Room setup, tubs, partner accommodation +

Mercy: LDRP private rooms (labor, delivery, recovery, postpartum all in one room). Partner sleep sofa. No tubs.

Willow Creek: Low-intervention birthing suites with tubs, wireless fetal monitoring so you can walk, birthing balls. Private rooms.

Washington Regional: Private rooms, dedicated twins rooms in NICU, partner accommodation. Ask about tubs.

Lactation support and breastfeeding +

Mercy: Best-documented lactation infrastructure in NWA. Outpatient lactation clinic that continues after you go home, "Weigh-In Wednesdays" drop-in, milk bank depot. Dedicated Perinatal Navigator for the weeks after.

Willow Creek: Inpatient IBCLC lactation consultants, postpartum support group, dedicated women's pelvic floor PT clinic.

Washington Regional: Certified lactation consultants + Infant Nutrition Lab (unique to them).

What happens if there's an emergency +

Mercy: 24/7 in-house OB + CRNA (fastest emergency response of the three). New OB Emergency Department. Level III NICU on-site. 5 minutes from home — the drive-time advantage matters most in precipitous labor or weather emergencies.

Willow Creek: Level IIIa NICU with specialized neonatal transport, high-frequency ventilation, therapeutic cooling. Anesthesia staffing model not publicly confirmed.

Washington Regional: Largest NICU in NWA, 6 neonatologists, only on-site MFM specialist. Best option for high-risk or complicated deliveries. Patient-initiated rapid response.

For any NWA hospital: The only Level IV NICU in the state is at Arkansas Children's in Little Rock. Mercy and Washington Regional have transfer relationships established. Arkansas Children's Northwest in Springdale doesn't have L&D but can serve as pediatric backup.

Postpartum mental health +

Mercy: Screening at discharge + Perinatal Navigator follow-up. No on-site psychiatry.

Willow Creek: Postpartum support groups. No documented specialist pathway.

Washington Regional: Only NWA facility with a named perinatal psychiatrist (Dr. Erin Jefferson, UAMS Women's Mental Health Program). Real specialist-level referral path if postpartum depression or anxiety emerges.

Postpartum depression affects about 1 in 7 new moms in the US, and the rate in Arkansas is higher. Worth planning for the option, even if you don't need it.

Insurance, billing, out-of-pocket +

Mercy: Native online cost estimator. Does NOT sue patients over unpaid bills. Most patient-friendly billing culture of the three.

Willow Creek: Uses MDSave pre-pay marketplace. May hold BCBS Blue Distinction+ for maternity — if Walmart routes through that, could be lowest out-of-pocket. Verify in benefits portal.

Washington Regional: Does sue patients over unpaid bills (Leapfrog-flagged). Otherwise standard billing.

For Walmart associates: Doula services are covered at $0 copay. Check the Walmart benefits portal for each facility's network tier before you decide.

Doula-friendliness +

Willow Creek: Strongest pro-doula stance. Cites ACOG directly. Makes doulas feel welcome as part of the team.

Mercy: Marketed as "doula-friendly birthplace." Welcoming in practice.

Washington Regional: Allows doulas; not prominently featured in marketing. No reports of friction.

VBAC (if we have another baby later) +

Willow Creek: First-in-nation VBAC Center of Excellence (self-designated, but backed by real infrastructure: CNMs + 24/7 OB + L3A NICU + lowest primary C-section rate).

Mercy & Washington Regional: Physician-led TOLAC (trial of labor after C-section) is possible but neither has a published VBAC-focused program.

Arkansas as a state has a very low VBAC rate — most women with a prior C-section default to repeat. Willow Creek is actively fighting that trend.
