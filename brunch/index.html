Walk-On's Style Cheese Grits

The real deal from Baton Rouge — creamy, cheesy, and rich enough to make you forget you're not on Nicholson Drive. This recipe takes Walk-On's double-corn technique and layers in sharp cheddar for the full experience.

The Walk-On's Secret

Walk-On's doesn't just use water — they cook grits in chicken broth and heavy cream. They also use a double-corn method: caramelized sweet kernel corn and creamed corn folded into the base. That's what makes theirs taste different from every other bowl of grits you've had.

Ingredients (serves 7-8)

Instructions

1
Caramelize the corn. Melt 2 tbsp butter in a skillet over medium-high. Add the sweet corn kernels and a pinch of sugar. Cook 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until golden and slightly caramelized. Set aside.
2
Heat the liquids. In a large heavy-bottomed pot, combine chicken broth, 2 cups of the heavy cream, and the milk. Bring to a gentle boil over medium heat.
3
Add the grits slowly. Whisk the stone-ground grits into the boiling liquid in a slow, steady stream. Keep whisking — no lumps allowed. Reduce heat to low.
4
Low and slow. Cover and simmer 20-25 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes. If they get too thick, add remaining heavy cream in splashes. You want them creamy, not stiff.
5
The double-corn fold. Stir in the creamed corn and the caramelized corn kernels. Cook another 3-4 minutes.
6
OFF heat, add the cheese and butter. Remove from heat. Stir in the remaining 4 tbsp butter and all 3 cups of sharp cheddar. The residual heat melts everything without the cheese breaking. Add salt, garlic powder, pepper, and cayenne.
7
Taste and adjust. Grits should be creamy, rich, and aggressively cheesy. Add more salt or cheese if needed. Top with sliced green onions.
Pro Tip: The cheese goes in OFF the heat. This is the #1 thing every top-rated recipe agrees on — if you add cheese while the pot is on the burner, it can separate and get grainy. Off heat, it melts into silk.
Pro Tip: Stone-ground grits are worth the extra simmer time. Quick grits will work in a pinch (5 min cook), but the texture is nowhere close. Look for "stone-ground" or "old fashioned" on the bag.

Parmesan Crusted Chicken

Boneless, skinless chicken breasts coated in a crispy Parmesan crust. Golden, crunchy, juicy inside.

Instructions

1
Preheat oven to 400°F. Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper or foil. Lightly spray with cooking spray.
2
Mix the crust. In a shallow dish, combine Parmesan, breadcrumbs, garlic powder, paprika, salt, and pepper.
3
Prep the chicken. If breasts are thick, pound them to even thickness (~3/4 inch) with a mallet or rolling pin. This ensures even cooking.
4
Coat. Dip each chicken breast in beaten egg, then press firmly into the Parmesan breadcrumb mixture on both sides. Place on the baking sheet.
5
Drizzle. Drizzle or brush melted butter over the top of each piece. This helps the crust get golden and crispy.
6
Bake 20-25 minutes until internal temp reaches 165°F and the crust is golden brown. No flipping needed.
7
Rest 5 minutes before serving. Garnish with fresh parsley if you have it.
Pro Tip: Use two sheet pans so the chicken isn't crowded — crowding steams it instead of crisping it. Space them at least 1 inch apart.

Cheesy Parmesan Mashed Potatoes

Creamy, rich, loaded with Parmesan and butter. The perfect pairing with the crusted chicken.

Instructions

1
Boil the potatoes. Place cubed potatoes in a large pot, cover with cold water, add 1 tbsp salt. Bring to boil, cook 15-20 min until fork-tender. Drain well.
2
Melt butter + garlic. While potatoes cook, melt butter in a small saucepan. If using garlic, cook it in the butter for 1-2 min until fragrant (not browned).
3
Mash. Mash drained potatoes with a masher or hand mixer. Add the garlic butter and warm heavy cream. Mash until smooth and creamy.
4
Fold in Parmesan. Stir in the grated Parmesan until fully melted and incorporated. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
5
Serve. Transfer to a serving bowl. Top with extra Parmesan and chopped chives. Can be kept warm in a covered pot on lowest heat for up to 1 hour.
Pro Tip: Warm the cream before adding — cold cream into hot potatoes makes them gummy. Microwave it for 30 seconds or warm in a saucepan.

Deviled Eggs

1
Hard boil eggs. Place in pot, cover with cold water. Bring to boil, cover, remove from heat. Let sit 12 min. Ice bath to cool.
2
Peel and halve. Scoop yolks into a bowl.
3
Mix filling. Mash yolks with mayo, Dijon, vinegar, salt, pepper. Pipe or spoon back into whites.
4
Garnish. Sprinkle with paprika and chives. Cover and refrigerate — best made Saturday night.

Other Dinner Ideas (For Next Time)

Chicken

Ina Garten's Perfect Roast Chicken (x2) Low Effort

The most foolproof roast chicken on the internet. Two 4-5 lb whole chickens. Rinse, pat dry, stuff with lemon halves and thyme, tie the legs, rub with butter, salt and pepper generously. Roast at 425°F for 1 hour 15 min. That's it.

Why it works: Ina's recipe has thousands of 5-star reviews because it's dead simple — 5 ingredients, no basting, no flipping. The high heat gives crispy skin every time.

Feeds 7: Two chickens. About 15 min active prep, then the oven does the rest.

Pairs with: Roasted potatoes (toss around the chicken in the same pan), asparagus, dinner rolls.

Sheet Pan Lemon Herb Chicken & Potatoes Low Effort

14 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs on two sheet pans with baby potatoes, lemon wedges, and garlic cloves. Drizzle with olive oil, sprinkle rosemary, thyme, salt, pepper. 425°F for 45 minutes.

Why it works: One pan = one cleanup. Chicken thighs are forgiving — almost impossible to dry out. The potatoes roast in the chicken drippings.

Feeds 7: 2 thighs per person + potatoes. Use two sheet pans side by side.

Pairs with: Roasted asparagus (add to pans last 15 min), dinner rolls, green beans.

Sticky Apricot Glazed Chicken Low Effort

Bone-in chicken thighs or drumsticks glazed with apricot preserves, Dijon mustard, soy sauce, and garlic. Bake at 400°F for 40-45 min. The glaze caramelizes into a sweet, sticky coating.

Why it works: Looks impressive, tastes like you spent hours, but it's literally mix a glaze, pour it on chicken, bake. Kids love it too.

Feeds 7: 3-4 lbs chicken pieces. Double the glaze recipe if using two pans.

Pairs with: Rice, roasted carrots, sweet potato casserole.

Chicken & Rice Casserole Low Effort

Boneless chicken thighs, white rice, cream of mushroom soup, chicken broth, onion powder, garlic powder. Mix everything in a 9x13, lay chicken on top, cover with foil, bake at 375°F for 1 hour. Uncover last 15 min for browning.

Why it works: True dump-and-bake. Rice cooks in the casserole — no separate pot. One dish, one oven, done.

Feeds 7: Double batch in a deep 9x13 or use two pans.

Pairs with: Green beans, dinner rolls, a simple salad.

Sheet Pan BBQ Chicken with Corn & Potato Wedges Low Effort

Chicken thighs brushed with your favorite BBQ sauce, corn on the cob (halved), and potato wedges. All on one sheet pan. 400°F for 35-40 min. Brush more sauce halfway through.

Why it works: Entire meal on one pan. Kid-friendly. Use Sweet Baby Ray's or whatever BBQ sauce you already have.

Feeds 7: Two sheet pans, 14 thighs, 7 half-ears corn, 3 lbs potatoes.

Pairs with: Cornbread, coleslaw. The full cookout-meets-Easter vibe.

Lamb

Greek Lemon Roasted Leg of Lamb with Potatoes Medium Effort

Bone-in leg of lamb (5-6 lbs), stabbed with garlic slivers, rubbed with lemon juice, olive oil, oregano, salt, pepper. Surround with quartered potatoes. Roast at 350°F for about 2.5 hours (20 min per pound).

Why it works: Traditional Easter centerpiece in Greek and many European households. The potatoes absorb the lamb drippings and get unbelievably good. Active prep is 15 minutes — the oven does the rest.

Feeds 7: 5-6 lb bone-in leg. Figure 3/4 lb per person with bone.

Pairs with: Greek salad, tzatziki, pita bread, roasted asparagus.

Herb-Crusted Rack of Lamb Medium Effort

Two racks of lamb (8 ribs each), coated with Dijon mustard then pressed with a garlic-rosemary-breadcrumb crust. Sear 2 min per side, then roast at 450°F for 15-20 min (medium-rare).

Why it works: Looks like a restaurant dish but actual cook time is under 25 minutes. The mustard acts as glue for the herb crust. This is the "impress everyone" option.

Feeds 7: Two full racks (16 ribs total, about 2 per person). Pricier cut — budget ~$40-50 for the lamb.

Pairs with: Mashed potatoes, roasted asparagus, mint sauce, dinner rolls.

Braised Lamb Shanks Medium Effort

7 lamb shanks, seared in a Dutch oven, then braised low and slow with red wine, beef broth, carrots, onion, garlic, tomato paste, and rosemary. 325°F for 2.5-3 hours covered.

Why it works: Braising is hands-off after the initial sear. The meat falls off the bone. Rich, hearty, and feels like a special occasion. Can be made a day ahead and reheated (gets better overnight).

Feeds 7: One shank per person. Check Walmart meat counter for availability.

Pairs with: Mashed potatoes (to soak up the sauce), crusty bread, roasted vegetables.

Beef

Prime Rib Roast Medium Effort

Standing rib roast (4-5 ribs, about 8-10 lbs bone-in). Season generously with salt, pepper, garlic, and herbs the night before. Let it come to room temp for 1 hour. Roast at 500°F for 15 min to sear, then drop to 325°F until internal temp hits 130°F for medium-rare (about 2-2.5 hours). Rest 20 min before carving.

Why it works: Simpler than it sounds — it's just a big roast. The reverse-sear method is foolproof if you use a meat thermometer. Show-stopping on the table.

Feeds 7: Figure 1 lb per person bone-in. 4-rib roast (~8 lbs) handles 7 easily.

Budget: This is the splurge option — expect $60-80+ at Walmart for a prime rib.

Pairs with: Mashed potatoes, horseradish cream, Yorkshire pudding, roasted asparagus.

Beef Tenderloin Medium Effort

Whole beef tenderloin (3-4 lbs trimmed), tied with kitchen twine. Season with salt, pepper, garlic. Sear all sides in a hot skillet (2 min per side), then roast at 425°F for 20-25 min until 130°F internal. Rest 10 min, slice into medallions.

Why it works: The most tender cut of beef. Cooks fast. Slices into beautiful medallions that look like a steakhouse served them. Total cook time under 40 min.

Feeds 7: 3-4 lb tenderloin (about 6 oz per person).

Budget: Another splurge — ~$50-70 at Walmart.

Pairs with: Roasted potatoes, green beans almondine, dinner rolls, peppercorn sauce.

Pork (Not Ham)

Apricot Glazed Pork Tenderloin Low Effort

Two pork tenderloins (about 1 lb each), rubbed with salt, pepper, garlic powder. Sear in a hot skillet 2 min per side, then brush with a glaze of apricot preserves, Dijon mustard, and soy sauce. Roast at 400°F for 20-25 min until internal temp hits 145°F.

Why it works: Pork tenderloin is cheap, lean, and cooks in 25 minutes. The sweet-tangy glaze makes it Easter-worthy. Under 30 minutes total.

Feeds 7: Two 1 lb tenderloins. Slice into medallions.

Pairs with: Mashed potatoes, roasted asparagus, dinner rolls, sweet potato casserole.

Brown Sugar Garlic Pork Chops Low Effort

7 bone-in pork chops (1 per person), seared 3 min per side, then baked at 400°F for 15 min with a brown sugar, garlic, butter, and paprika glaze.

Why it works: Each person gets their own chop — no carving needed. The glaze caramelizes beautifully. Done in under 25 minutes.

Feeds 7: 7 bone-in chops. Buy them thick-cut (1 inch+) so they stay juicy.

Pairs with: Mac & cheese, roasted green beans, cornbread, mashed potatoes.

Seafood

Sheet Pan Lemon Herb Salmon with Asparagus Low Effort

Large salmon fillet (2.5-3 lbs) or 7 individual portions on a sheet pan with asparagus. Drizzle with olive oil, lemon juice, minced garlic, dill, salt, pepper. 400°F for 15-20 minutes.

Why it works: Fastest protein on this list — 20 minutes from oven to table. Light and fresh for spring. One pan, almost zero cleanup.

Feeds 7: 3 lb fillet or 7 individual 6-oz portions.

Pairs with: Rice, roasted potatoes, Caesar salad, dinner rolls.

Cajun / Southern

Cajun Chicken & Sausage Jambalaya (No Shrimp) Medium Effort

One-pot: diced chicken thighs, sliced andouille sausage, the Cajun trinity (onion, celery, bell pepper), diced tomatoes, chicken broth, and long-grain rice. Season with Cajun seasoning, thyme, bay leaves. Bring to boil, cover, simmer 25 min.

Why it works: One pot feeds a crowd. Ties into the Walk-On's brunch theme — Louisiana all day. NO SHRIMP in this version. Rich, filling, and reheats great.

Feeds 7: One large Dutch oven or stock pot. Double if using a smaller pot.

Pairs with: Cornbread, simple green salad. Honestly it's a complete meal on its own.

Easter Sides — Pick 2-3

Deviled eggs are already happening. Pick 2-3 more from below to round out the plate.

Deviled Eggs (Classic Easter) LOCKED IN

1 dozen eggs, hard boiled, halved. Mix yolks with mayo, Dijon mustard, white vinegar, salt, pepper, and a pinch of paprika. Pipe or spoon filling back into whites. Sprinkle with paprika and fresh chives.

Pro move: Make these Saturday night — they're actually better after sitting in the fridge overnight. Cover with plastic wrap.

Feeds 7: 1 dozen = 24 halves, about 3 per person.

Walmart: Eggs, mayo, Dijon mustard, paprika, chives.

Southern Baked Mac & Cheese Low Effort

1 lb elbow macaroni, 3 cups shredded sharp cheddar, 8 oz Velveeta cubed, 2 eggs, 1 can evaporated milk, 4 tbsp butter, salt, pepper, paprika. Cook pasta, mix everything in a 9x13, top with extra cheese. Bake at 350°F for 35-40 min.

Why it works: Velveeta gives the creamy base, sharp cheddar gives the flavor. This is not box mac — this is the real thing. Feeds a crowd from one pan.

Feeds 7: 9x13 pan, easily 8-10 servings.

Roasted Asparagus Low Effort

2 bunches asparagus, trimmed. Toss with olive oil, salt, pepper, and minced garlic. Single layer on a sheet pan. 400°F for 12-15 minutes. Optional: squeeze of lemon and shaved Parmesan when it comes out.

Why it works: 15 minutes, one pan, almost no prep. The quintessential spring vegetable. Looks elegant on the plate.

Feeds 7: 2 bunches (about 2 lbs total).

Creamy Mashed Potatoes Low Effort

3 lbs Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled, cubed, boiled until fork-tender (15-20 min). Drain, mash with 1 stick butter, 1/2 cup heavy cream, salt, pepper. Mash until smooth or leave slightly chunky.

Why it works: Yukon Golds are naturally buttery and creamy. No need for a ricer — a regular masher works fine. Can be made 2 hours ahead, kept warm in a covered pot on lowest heat.

Feeds 7: 3 lbs potatoes, about 1/2 lb per person.

Sweet Potato Casserole Low Effort

4 lbs sweet potatoes, boiled and mashed. Mix with 1/2 cup brown sugar, 1/4 cup melted butter, 1/3 cup milk, 2 eggs, 1 tsp vanilla, 1/2 tsp cinnamon. Spread in a 9x13. Top with a mix of 1 cup pecans, 1/3 cup flour, 1/3 cup brown sugar, 3 tbsp melted butter. Bake 375°F for 25 min.

Why it works: Make-ahead friendly — assemble Saturday, bake Sunday. The pecan crumble topping is what makes it. Can also do marshmallow topping if the kids prefer.

Feeds 7: 9x13 pan, 8-10 servings easily.

Roasted Green Beans with Garlic Low Effort

2 lbs fresh green beans, trimmed. Toss with olive oil, 4 minced garlic cloves, salt, pepper. Roast at 425°F for 20 minutes. Optional: toss with toasted slivered almonds when done (green beans almondine).

Why it works: Simple, fresh, healthy. Roasting gives them a slight char that steaming can't match. The garlic makes them irresistible.

Feeds 7: 2 lbs, about 4-5 oz per person.

Honey Roasted Carrots Low Effort

2 lbs whole carrots (or baby carrots), tossed with 2 tbsp melted butter, 1 tbsp honey, salt, pepper, and fresh thyme. Roast at 400°F for 25-30 min until caramelized and tender.

Why it works: Sweet, colorful, and roasting brings out natural sugars. Kids who won't eat vegetables will eat these. Almost zero prep.

Feeds 7: 2 lbs carrots on one sheet pan.

Dinner Rolls Low Effort

Easy route: Pillsbury Crescent Rolls or Hawaiian Sweet Rolls from the bakery section. Pop open, bake per instructions, done in 12 min.

From scratch: Sally's Baking Addiction soft dinner rolls — flour, yeast, butter, milk, sugar, salt. Mix, rise 1 hour, shape into balls, rise 30 min more, bake 20 min at 350°F. Makes 15 rolls.

Feeds 7: 2 rolls per person. Buy or make 15 to have extras.

Cornbread Low Effort

Jiffy Corn Muffin Mix (2 boxes) or from scratch: 1 cup cornmeal, 1 cup flour, 1/3 cup sugar, 1 tbsp baking powder, 1/2 tsp salt, 1 egg, 1 cup milk, 1/3 cup melted butter. Mix, pour in a greased cast iron or 8x8 pan. Bake 400°F for 20-25 min.

Why it works: Southern staple. Pairs with literally everything on this page. Jiffy mix makes it a 5-minute prep. Goes great with the Cajun jambalaya option.

Feeds 7: One pan, cut into squares.

Scalloped Potatoes Medium Effort

3 lbs russet potatoes, thinly sliced. Layer in a greased 9x13 with a sauce of 3 tbsp butter, 3 tbsp flour, 2.5 cups milk, 2 cups shredded Gruyere or cheddar, salt, pepper, garlic. Pour sauce over layers. Top with extra cheese. Cover and bake at 350°F for 1 hour, uncover last 15 min for browning.

Why it works: Creamy, cheesy, and impressive on the table. The slicing takes a few minutes but after that it's all oven time. Can be assembled a few hours ahead.

Feeds 7: 9x13 pan, 8-10 servings.

Simple Caesar Salad Low Effort

2 heads romaine, chopped. Toss with store-bought Caesar dressing (Cardini's or Ken's), croutons, and shaved Parmesan. Done.

Why it works: Fresh, crunchy, takes 5 minutes, and balances out heavier mains. Buy a bag of Caesar salad kit if you want it even easier.

Feeds 7: 2 heads romaine or 2 salad kits.
